# The Relief

A one-shot for four 3rd-level characters. Play-by-post, theater of the mind, no map, no miniatures.
Three to four hours of real time, or a week of slow posting. Written to be someone's first game.

---

## The pitch, in the words you would use at the table

The weather station at Cairn Head is manned by three people and relieved on the first of the month. The relief
launch left Ardmore at four. It has not arrived. The storm came in at six. The radio has been saying the same
nine words since seven, and the words are not the words a radio should say.

You are the relief crew. You are in a boat that cannot turn back, in weather that is going to get worse, going out
to a rock with a light on it that stopped answering an hour ago.

**Why the characters go:** it is the job, and it is four hours to the nearest other people. This is a one-shot
about a place, a storm, and a decision - not about being heroes.

---

## What the players do not know, and you do

The station's three keepers found something in the tide-line two days ago: a shipping container, holed, the lock
still on it, and inside it forty crates of medical supplies that were reported lost in the *Ravenscraig* sinking
in March. Nine people died going down with that ship. Somebody moved the container off the wreck, brought it here,
and left it in a cove where it would wash up on the station's own doorstep, so the station would find it and not
report it.

Two of the keepers want to sell it. One of them, **Mira Halloran**, locked herself in the radio room at seven and
has been transmitting on the emergency channel since - not words, a *tone*. She is not being held prisoner. She is
holding the other two at a distance with the only leverage she has: if she keeps transmitting, somebody will come.
Which is what has brought you.

The two who want to sell it, **Alec Brae** and **Tomas Rennie**, have spent four hours deciding what to do about
her, and have run out of options that do not involve you.

---

## The clock

The storm is the clock. Mark it in the fiction, not on a sheet: after **four scenes**, the swell is too big for the
launch to hold position and the boat's coxswain (NPC, and the only way off the rock) says he is leaving in one
hour, crew or no crew. **Tell the players this when the fourth scene ends.** One of them can talk him into staying
(DC 15 Charisma, or DC 12 with a reason he believes), and it costs them the hour.

If they are still on the rock when he goes, there is the station's own boat: a fourteen-foot punt, seaworthy, and it
takes six hours to row back in this. The game does not end. It gets harder, and everyone knows it.

---

## The station, in five places

**1. The landing.** A slipway cut into the rock, a winch, and the launch. The coxswain is **Danny Vane**, 60s,
uninterested in anyone's trouble, will wait out of professional pride and no other reason. There is a container
crane's hook scars on the slip, fresh, and a length of chain that does not belong to the winch.

**2. The mess.** Where you will do most of this. A stove, a table, four chairs and five mugs. Alec Brae is here,
drinking, and he is the one who talks. There is a tide table on the wall with two days circled, and a logbook whose
last honest entry is three days old.

**3. The radio room, locked.** Mira inside, a chair wedged under the handle from her side. The tone on the
emergency channel is the station's own weather-drum, a mechanical thing that reports wind speed - she has pointed it
at the microphone. It is not a distress call. It is a way of saying *someone is coming* without saying anything a
court could use.

**4. The transmitter shed.** Behind the station, unlocked, and where the actual crime is: the station's main radio
has been opened and its emergency battery removed. Whoever did it wanted the *official* distress channel dead and
the drum left, which took knowing the difference. **Tomas Rennie** did it, on Alec's instruction, and he is sitting
in the shed with his hands in his coat because he cannot think of anywhere else to be.

**5. The cove.** Ten minutes down a wet path, and where the container is, half in the water on its side, crates
spilled, one broken open - dressings, saline, morphine in glass. The lock has been cut *from outside* since it was
brought here, and the cut is fresh. Somebody else knows.

---

## The three keepers

- **Alec Brae**, 40s, station lead, the one who talks and the one who did the arithmetic. He is not a monster and
  should not be played as one: he is a man who has spent two years on a rock and did the sum on what the supplies
  are worth against what the ship already cost. **Wants:** the crew to take the supplies, the radio to stay broken,
  and Mira to be someone else's problem. **Will:** offer the party a share within ten minutes of meeting them, and
  offer it warmly.
- **Tomas Rennie**, 20s, the junior keeper. Did the cutting and the removing and has not slept. **Wants:** for
  somebody to tell him what to do. **Will:** fold the moment a character speaks to him without threat, and tell them
  everything, including the part Alec has not said out loud - that the *Ravenscraig* went down in water the station
  keeps the charts for, and Alec was on the radio that night.
- **Mira Halloran**, 30s, the one in the radio room. **Wants:** witnesses. She has been transmitting for an hour to
  make the station impossible to explain away, and she will not open the door to anyone until she has heard a voice
  she does not recognise. **Will:** open it for the party, and stay behind them for the rest of the game.

---

## Scenes, and what to roll

**Scene 1 - the landing.** Getting off the launch in a swell: **DC 12 Dex (Acrobatics)** or take 4 damage and
lose a pack. Then the hook scars and the chain, if someone looks: **DC 13 Wis (Perception)** sees them; **DC 15 Int
(Investigation)** works out they are container-crane scars and not winch wear. This is where a party decides
whether it is curious.

**Scene 2 - the mess, and Alec.** He has a story ready: the storm, the radio, Mira being unwell. It is *mostly
true*, which is why it is hard to break. **Insight DC 15** catches the one place he will not be pushed - what the
last logbook entry says. If a character reads the logbook while he talks, **DC 12** is enough to see three days
missing and a page cut out with a knife. He will not attack. He will offer money, and he will keep offering.

**Scene 3 - the shed, and Tomas.** He talks. No roll needed if they are kind; **Intimidation** gets the same
information and costs them the ending where he testifies. He gives them the *Ravenscraig*, and the charts, and the
fact that the station's main set was opened from inside with the station's own tools.

**Scene 4 - the cove.** The container, the cut lock, the morphine. And the fourth party: **a boat's light, a mile
out and not moving**, which has been there long enough to be deliberate. Whoever cut the lock is waiting for the
weather to turn. **Perception DC 14** spots it; without it, they learn about it in Scene 5 the hard way.

**Scene 5 - the hour.** Danny on the launch, the light on the water, the rain. This is the scene the game is for
and it should be played slowly. Everything above is now a decision about what four people do with an hour and a
radio.

---

## How it ends

There is no right answer and the ending should come out of the argument, not out of a die.

- **Witnesses.** Mira's tone and the party's word get the logbook, the charts and Tomas up the hill. Alec is
  arrested on the mainland. The supplies go back into the record. The party gets a court date, a storm story, and
  nothing else. *(The honest ending. It is also the one that costs them the most time, and the one where the boat
  light comes in and finds four people standing over a cut lock with no story.)*
- **Cut in.** They take Alec's share, or take the supplies themselves, and leave on Danny's launch with the crates.
  The light on the water follows them to the harbour mouth and stops. Nobody says anything about it. *(This ending
  should be played without judgement. It is what the job pays, and the game is not a morality play - but Tomas'
  face as they load is worth describing in full.)*
- **The fourth party.** If they found the light and chose to meet it, the people who cut the lock are not criminals
  but the *Ravenscraig*'s insurers' agents, who have been waiting for the container to be moved a second time so
  they could see who moved it. This ending gives the party everything and takes the decision out of their hands,
  which is exactly why it should only happen if they go looking for it.
- **The punt.** If Danny leaves, the game does not end - it moves to the station for six hours with the storm
  rising. Run the same argument with no exit, and let Mira's radio be the only thing that might reach anyone.

## Four premade characters, 3rd level

Level 3, standard array (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8), no feats, no multiclassing, and **written so that a player who
has never rolled a d20 can start in five minutes.** Each has one reason to be on the boat and one thing they are
good at.

**Species: any, and put the ability increases wherever the character is already good.** The numbers below are the
array with those increases already applied, which is why they read 16 rather than 15. Nobody needs to choose a
species to sit down - if a player wants one, it changes nothing in this session except what they look like.

| | **Nell Aird** | **Fionn Brae** | **Sadie Okonkwo** | **Rab Malloy** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Role | the one who was there before | the one with a brother on the rock | the one who was hired | the one who does not say |
| Class | Cleric (Life), 3 | Ranger (Hunter), 3 | Rogue (Thief), 3 | Fighter (Champion), 3 |
| STR/DEX/CON | 12 / 10 / 14 | 12 / 16 / 14 | 10 / 16 / 12 | 16 / 14 / 15 |
| INT/WIS/CHA | 10 / 16 / 12 | 10 / 14 / 8 | 13 / 12 / 14 | 9 / 12 / 10 |
| Why here | she worked this station for six months and left | her brother is one of the three keepers | she was paid by the *Ravenscraig*'s owners to look and not touch | he was on the *Ravenscraig* the night it went down |
| The one thing | she knows how the station is laid out, and which door sticks | she finds things - Perception and the tide tables | she opens things - locks, crates, and the radio room if it comes to it | he survives things, and he has been holding a name for two years |

**Fionn is Tomas' sister and does not know he is on the rock.** Nell knows Mira and knows why she would be the one
in the radio room. Sadie's employer is the "fourth party", which means the players may work out that one of them
is being paid by the people waiting on the water - the best scene in the one-shot, and it depends on Sadie's player
being up for it. Rab was on the *Ravenscraig*; he has not said so, and the charts on the wall of the station are the
same ones he saw that night.

## DM notes, honestly

- **This is a mystery with no combat in it, and that is a choice.** D&D runs fine without initiative for one
  session, and a table of new players learns more from a locked door, a hostile conversation and a die roll with a
  stake than from an owlbear. If your table wants a fight, Tomas' panic in the shed is the only honest one, and it
  should be a chase down a wet path, not a standoff.
- **The dice are for the four scenes above; the argument is the game.** If a roll is not going to change what
  happens next, do not ask for it.
- **Say the weather out loud, every scene.** Wind speed, the drum, the swell, the dark. The storm is the clock and
  the mood and the reason nobody can just leave.
- **Let them win the argument they choose to have.** All four endings are endings. Nothing here has a correct
  answer, and the one thing that will make the session land is the DM not having one either.
