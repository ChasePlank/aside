Put Testimony on a phone, and move the prose out of the screen so it can

The fourth code game with a single-file web build, after Vigil, Residue and
Ledger. Testimony is the first of them whose whole shape is a conversation
rather than a room or a ledger: it asks you eight questions and then reads
your answers back as settled fact, so the phone build has to carry the
interview itself and not just a rendering of it.

WHAT IS NOT DUPLICATED. The template
(src/main/resources/testimony/web.html) holds the layout and the rules. The
content -- the evening, the eight questions, their options and phrases, the
account lines, the four closings, the tally sentences and every heading the
game says in its own voice -- is generated from Testimony.java, the same
class the engine screen reads.

That last part was new work. The headings and the closing sentences were
string literals inside TestimonyScreen; they moved into the model on this
fire and the screen now reads them, so there is one copy of each sentence
and the two builds cannot drift. The closings became templates carrying
%wrong% / %plural% / %sure% rather than concatenation, for the same reason:
the phone build fills them, and it must not own them. SelfTest.closings()
covers the four branches and the singular, which is exactly the case a
second copy of a sentence gets wrong.

WHAT IS WRITTEN TWICE. The interview's arithmetic: eight questions in a
fixed order, an answer written into memory whether or not it was true,
nothing anywhere recording which, propagation into the phrasing of later
questions, and the four-way choice of closing. About sixty lines of
JavaScript, checked rather than trusted -- SelfTest regenerates this file
and fails if the checked-in copy has gone stale.

Verified by driving both builds through the same four playthroughs and
diffing what they say: the account, the tally and the closing are identical
in all four. The propagation was checked in the phone build directly -- say
the coat was blue and the last question asks about the woman in the blue
coat. The returning player lands on the verdict, not on the evening again.
All five phases rendered in Chrome at 390x844, and the desktop screen
re-rendered to confirm the constant move did not change it.

The shelf picked it up without being told, which is the orphan check earning
its keep: 6 builds, 206 KB. Engine suite 88 -> 92 checks.

WHAT THE PORT FOUND, and did not fix. Question.note() is written for all
eight questions -- eight paragraphs, and some of the best writing in the
file -- and nothing has ever drawn it. Not the screen, not the verdict, not
this build. It is left undrawn rather than drawn on one build only, because
a note that appears on the phone and not on the desktop is a divergence
dressed up as a feature. The verdict is where it belongs, and the desktop
verdict has about 90px of canvas left below the closing, so drawing eight
notes there is a layout change and not a one-line change. Recorded.
