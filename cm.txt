Add Lesson, where a state only ever teaches its own corner

The fifteenth verb is teaching, and it is the one the other fourteen
assume. Residue leaves, Ledger records, Testimony recalls, Handoff hands
over, Outside tells, Bearings checks, Inventory names, Vigil keeps,
Corroboration believes, Attribution credits, Drift compares, Redaction
bars, Relay carries, Interval waits -- and every one of them is about
what happens to something after somebody already knows how to do it.
This one is about the part before that.

You have run a boiler for eleven years and in three nights you hand it
to somebody else. Two gauges and a vent, and the rule is one line: you
open the vent when the pressure is up. You have never had to say why,
because you have never had to tell anybody.

The rule the whole thing rests on: a state only ever teaches its own
corner. The board has four of them -- pressure up or down, heat up or
down -- and a state in one corner says nothing whatever about the other
three. Three nights spent in one corner is one corner taught three
times, and the student's belief is exactly as wide after the third night
as it was after the first. That is the failure the game exists to make
legible: it does not look like a failure while it is happening. Every
demonstration feels like teaching, the student keeps saying something,
and the nights keep going.

The eight rules the student considers are declared, and so is the order
they try them in, so their belief is a fact about the demonstrations
rather than a guess about the student. No two of the eight agree on all
four corners, which is why a demonstration in a new corner always
narrows the field and a demonstration in a corner they have already seen
never does -- and the game says so, on the screen, while it is still
happening, because the whole difficulty is that it does not feel like
one.

Measured: covering the shift's three corners is safe on 2000 of 2000
seeds and leaves the student certain about half the time. Showing the
first three states on the board is safe 66.5% of the time, which is the
number that says the game is not free. Three nights in one corner are
safe 30% of the time, which is the number that says the trap is real.

Lesson suite 624 checks. Engine suite 170. The phone build deals the
same board and the same shift as the desktop over 200 seeds, and agrees
about the standing set after the same three nights -- which is the
number the game turns on, and the one a ported model could get wrong on
both sides at once without either side noticing.
tools/lesson-play.mjs plays the shipped file through its own buttons,
including the claim the rule rests on.
